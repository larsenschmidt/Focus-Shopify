## The method · retrieval, not repetition
1. Shape first. Read aloud five times. Outline the argument in five words per section. Mark the sense units, the breath-sized clauses.
2. Snowball. Learn unit one: read once, close, say it; look only when stuck; three clean, add unit two, say both from the start. Always from the start.
3. First letters. Write the first letter of every word. Recite from the letters. Twice clean, fold the sheet away.
4. Three tiers, daily. New: today's units. Recent: this week's piece, whole. Old: everything known, one piece a day on rotation.
5. The schedule. At learning, one hour later, before sleep, on waking, then at three days, seven, fourteen, thirty. Then monthly, for life.
6. The gate. Clean three times across two separate days, standing, at speaking pace. Then mark it **known** with the date.
7. Then delivery. Pauses where a speaker would pause. Say it to a wall as if the wall were tired. Record once a week.

Load: one piece a week in a strong language, 150–300 words; half that in the weaker ones.

Palaces (`palace.md`, `components.md`) hold the order of units for long pieces, the points of a talk, and Chinese characters. Never the words.
