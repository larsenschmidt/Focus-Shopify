# The fourteen exercises · exact forms

The progymnasmata are forms, not moods. Phase I cycles 1–6 on weekdays. Phase II adds 7–12. Phase III adds 13–14. The form is a cage and the cage is the point.

1. **Fable.** Retell a fable (Aesop) in your own words, 150–250 words, then one sentence of moral. Variations: expand with dialogue; compress to 60 words; moral first.
2. **Narrative.** One event. Who, what, when, where, how, why, all present. 250–400 words. Tell it straight; once a month, in reverse.
3. **Chreia.** A saying or deed attributed to a person. Eight heads, 300 words: praise the one who said it; paraphrase it; the cause, why it is true; the contrary, what happens when it is ignored; a comparison; one example with a name; a witness, someone else who said the same; one sentence of conclusion.
4. **Maxim.** The same eight heads on a proverb with no speaker. The praise goes to the saying, not a person.
5. **Refutation.** Take a story or claim and argue it is unclear, implausible, impossible, inconsistent, improper or inexpedient. Pick three. 300–400 words.
6. **Confirmation.** The same claim argued clear, plausible, possible, consistent, proper, expedient. Pick three. 300–400 words.
7. **Commonplace.** Attack a vice in general, never a person: the liar, the idler. Heads: the contrary, exposition, comparison, intention, rejection of pity, legality, justice, expediency.
8. **Encomium.** Praise. Origin, upbringing, deeds of body, mind and fortune, comparison, epilogue.
9. **Invective.** The same heads, blame.
10. **Comparison.** Two things through the encomium heads side by side, ending in a judgment.
11. **Characterization.** A speech in the voice of a person at a moment: what would X say when Y. Present, then past, then future, in that order.
12. **Description.** Make the reader see: a person, place, time or event, from its parts, in order. No judgment until the last sentence.
13. **Thesis.** A general question, not a particular one: should one marry, not should I marry. Both sides in full, then a stand.
14. **Law.** For or against a proposed law: legality, justice, expediency, practicability.
